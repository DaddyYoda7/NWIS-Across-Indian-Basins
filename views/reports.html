01 / Executive Subsurface Diagnosis

The active borehole is currently drilling at 2,810 m MD through the lower Tipam sandstone transition, approaching the overpressured Barail coal-shale pay horizon (3,050–3,571 m). The primary operational hazard is intersection with natural fracture swarms associated with the Burhi Dihing fault and Naga thrust front. In the analog discovery well Nahorkatiya NHK-01 (DDR #42), entering this stratigraphic horizon caused an immediate standpipe pressure drop of 460 psi and 420 bbl total mud loss in 18 minutes.

Mandated Pre-Drill Action: Restrict dynamic ECD to ≤1.34 SG (cushion of 0.04 SG below 1.38 SG formation fracture gradient). Pre-stage 60 bbl fibrous/nut-plug LCM pill in auxiliary slug pit prior to crossing 2,840 m MD.

03 / Historical Discovery Benchmark Correlation DGH National Repository
1. Nahorkatiya Discovery Well No. 1 (1952–1953) • Upper Assam Basin

Spudded May 26, 1952; TD 3,571 m (11,715 ft); first successful alluvial plain reflection seismic discovery in Asia. Struck oil on June 16, 1953 in the lowest Barail sand reservoir (10,150–10,160 ft), flowing 580 bopd (32.4° API, 31°C pour point). Encountered major fracture loss (420 bbl) at 2,862m MD.

2. Mumbai High Discovery Well H-1-1 (1974) • Western Offshore Basin

Spudded Feb 3, 1974 in 62 m water by jackup Sagar Samrat. Drilling break at 962 m in Miocene L-III limestone with cavernous loss (520 bbl) and simultaneous gas kick. Flowed 2,400 bopd (39.5° API, 0.15% sulfur). Established India's largest petroleum field producing >500 MMT cumulative crude.

3. Digboi Well No. 1 (1889–1890) • Asia's First Commercial Oil Well

Spudded Sept 1889 by Assam Railways & Trading Co; completed Nov 1890 at 662 ft (202 m) TD using steam walking-beam cable percussion rig. Discovered via elephant trail oil seepages near Dibrugarh. Led to commissioning of the historic Digboi Refinery in 1901.
